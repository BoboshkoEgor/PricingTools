eSIM economics: margin, breakage, competitiveness and repricing¶

Travel eSIM is a different pricing problem from security software: every gigabyte has a real supplier cost that varies by destination, the market is a price-per-GB comparison game, and margin depends on how much data customers actually use. This notebook simulates a year of eSIM sales and walks through the analysis a pricing analyst would run.

Key formulas

What Formula
Net revenue per plan $P \cdot (1 - \text{channel fee})$ (prices ex-VAT)
Data cost, supplier bills per GB used $c_{GB} \cdot \text{GB} \cdot u$, where $u$ = utilization
Data cost, supplier bills per GB sold $c_{GB} \cdot \text{GB}$
Gross margin (on price) $\dfrac{P - \text{channel fees} - \text{data cost}}{P}$
Breakage $1 - u$ — data paid for but not used
Price index vs competitors our price per GB ÷ competitor median price per GB (same destination and plan size)
Floor price for a target margin $m^{*}$ $P_{min} = \dfrac{\text{expected data cost}}{1-\text{fee}-m^{*}}$

def plan_margin(price, gb, cost_per_gb, utilization, fee, billing="used"):
    net = price * (1 - fee)
    data_gb = gb * utilization if billing == "used" else gb
    cost = data_gb * cost_per_gb
    return pd.Series({"price": price, "channel fees": price - net, "data cost": cost,
                      "gross profit": net - cost, "gross margin": (net - cost) / price})

ex = pd.DataFrame({
    "billed per GB used": plan_margin(24.99, 10, 1.20, 0.70, 0.06, "used"),
    "billed per GB sold": plan_margin(24.99, 10, 1.20, 0.70, 0.06, "sold"),
})

Unused data (breakage) is pure margin in the first case and worth nothing in the second. Knowing the billing basis of each supplier contract is step one.

2. Simulate destinations, plans and a year of orders¶

Built-in assumptions, so we know what the analysis should find:

  • 12 destinations with very different wholesale costs per GB (cheap in Western Europe and Turkey, expensive in the UAE, Egypt and Japan) and different supplier billing bases.
  • Plans of 1, 3, 5, 10 and 20 GB. Bigger plans are cheaper per GB; small plans are used almost fully, big plans much less.
  • Our price positioning varies by destination (index vs competitors from about 0.85 to 1.25) — the result of years of ad-hoc pricing.
  • Channels: web store (6% payment fees) and mobile app (15% app-store commission plus payments).
  • Some customers top up with extra data, more often after small plans.

rng = np.random.default_rng(11)

dest = pd.DataFrame({
    "destination": ["Turkey", "France", "Italy", "Spain", "UK", "USA", "Japan", "Thailand", "UAE", "Egypt", "Brazil", "Indonesia"],
    "region":      ["Europe", "Europe", "Europe", "Europe", "Europe", "Americas", "Asia", "Asia", "Middle East", "Middle East", "Americas", "Asia"],
    "cost_per_gb": [0.55, 0.45, 0.50, 0.45, 0.60, 1.40, 1.60, 0.90, 3.20, 2.40, 1.90, 1.10],   # USD, wholesale
    "billing":     ["used", "used", "used", "sold", "used", "sold", "used", "used", "sold", "used", "sold", "used"],
    "comp_per_gb_10": [1.30, 1.10, 1.15, 1.10, 1.35, 2.60, 2.90, 1.60, 4.60, 3.40, 3.10, 1.90],  # competitor median, 10 GB plan
    "our_index":   [1.10, 1.25, 1.20, 1.05, 1.15, 0.90, 0.95, 1.05, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95, 1.00],
    "demand":      [0.14, 0.10, 0.09, 0.09, 0.08, 0.10, 0.07, 0.10, 0.07, 0.05, 0.05, 0.06],
})
plans = pd.DataFrame({
    "gb": [1, 3, 5, 10, 20],
    "plan_share": [0.15, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0.10],
    "util_mean": [0.93, 0.86, 0.76, 0.63, 0.48],
    "topup_prob": [0.35, 0.24, 0.15, 0.08, 0.04],
})

def comp_per_gb(c10, gb):                       # smaller plans cost more per GB
    return c10 * (gb / 10) ** -0.25

def pretty(p):                                  # round up to .99
    return np.ceil(p) - 0.01

price_list = dest.merge(plans[["gb"]], how="cross")
price_list["comp_price"] = comp_per_gb(price_list["comp_per_gb_10"], price_list["gb"]) * price_list["gb"]
price_list["price"] = pretty(price_list["comp_price"] * price_list["our_index"] * rng.normal(1, 0.03, len(price_list)))
price_list = price_list[["destination", "gb", "price", "comp_price"]]

N = 60_000
o = pd.DataFrame({
    "order_id": np.arange(1, N + 1),
    "month": rng.integers(1, 13, N),
    "destination": rng.choice(dest["destination"], N, p=dest["demand"]),
    "gb": rng.choice(plans["gb"], N, p=plans["plan_share"]),
    "channel": np.where(rng.random(N) < 0.65, "Web", "App"),
})
o = o.merge(dest[["destination", "region", "cost_per_gb", "billing"]], on="destination")
o = o.merge(price_list, on=["destination", "gb"]).merge(plans[["gb", "util_mean", "topup_prob"]], on="gb")
o["fee"] = np.where(o["channel"] == "Web", 0.06, 0.18)
o["refunded"] = rng.random(N) < 0.025                                   # activation problems, no data used
k = 12
o["utilization"] = np.where(o["refunded"], 0, rng.beta(o["util_mean"] * k, (1 - o["util_mean"]) * k))
o["topup_gb"] = np.where(~o["refunded"] & (rng.random(N) < o["topup_prob"]), rng.choice([1, 3], N, p=[.6, .4]), 0)

topup_price = price_list[price_list["gb"].isin([1, 3])].pivot(index="destination", columns="gb", values="price")
o["topup_revenue"] = [topup_price.loc[d, g] if g else 0.0 for d, g in zip(o["destination"], o["topup_gb"])]

o["gross_revenue"] = np.where(o["refunded"], 0, o["price"]) + o["topup_revenue"]      # ex-VAT, before channel fees
o["net_revenue"] = o["gross_revenue"] * (1 - o["fee"])
gb_billed = np.where(o["billing"] == "used", o["gb"] * o["utilization"], np.where(o["refunded"], 0, o["gb"]))
o["data_cost"] = (gb_billed + o["topup_gb"]) * o["cost_per_gb"]       # top-ups assumed fully used
o["gross_profit"] = o["net_revenue"] - o["data_cost"]

orders = o.drop(columns=["util_mean", "topup_prob"]).sort_values("order_id").reset_index(drop=True)
print(f"{len(orders):,} orders, revenue ${orders['gross_revenue'].sum():,.0f}, "
      f"gross margin {orders['gross_profit'].sum() / orders['gross_revenue'].sum():.1%}")

Big plans are used far less than small ones. Under per-GB-used billing that breakage keeps large plans about as profitable as small ones despite their much lower price per GB; under per-GB-sold billing, large plans become the least profitable because nothing offsets the low price per GB. Practical consequences: negotiate per-used billing where possible, and design the plan ladder (sizes and price steps) per supplier contract.

Read the chart in quadrants:

  • Right and high (expensive vs competitors, high margin — e.g. France, Italy): room to cut prices to win conversion, or keep as cash cows if conversion is fine.
  • Left and low (cheaper than competitors, thin margin — e.g. UAE, USA): underpriced given our costs — raise prices towards the market, or fix supplier costs.
  • Left and high: strong position — protect it.
  • Right and low: the worst place — expensive and unprofitable; usually a supplier-cost problem.

7. Supplier cost shock: which plans fall below the floor?¶

Scenario: the supplier raises wholesale costs by +20% in the USA, Egypt and Turkey. We recompute the expected margin of every plan at current prices and flag those below a 30% floor (gross margin on price).

FLOOR = 0.30
SHOCK = {"USA": 0.20, "Egypt": 0.20, "Turkey": 0.20}
avg_fee = (orders["fee"] * orders["gross_revenue"]).sum() / orders["gross_revenue"].sum()

pl = price_list.merge(dest[["destination", "cost_per_gb", "billing"]], on="destination").merge(plans[["gb", "util_mean"]], on="gb")
pl["cost_new"] = pl["cost_per_gb"] * (1 + pl["destination"].map(SHOCK).fillna(0))
billed = np.where(pl["billing"] == "used", pl["gb"] * pl["util_mean"], pl["gb"])
for tag, col in [("before", "cost_per_gb"), ("after", "cost_new")]:
    pl[f"exp_cost_{tag}"] = billed * pl[col]
    pl[f"margin_{tag}"] = 1 - avg_fee - pl[f"exp_cost_{tag}"] / pl["price"]
pl["below_floor_before"] = pl["margin_before"] < FLOOR
pl["below_floor_after"] = pl["margin_after"] < FLOOR

shock = pl[pl["destination"].isin(SHOCK)].copy()

CAP = 1.15   # don't price more than 15% above the competitor median

rec = pl.copy()
rec["floor_price"] = pretty(rec["exp_cost_after"] / (1 - avg_fee - FLOOR))
rec["cap_price"] = np.floor(rec["comp_price"] * CAP) - 0.01

    ax.barh(cnt.index, cnt[col], left=left, color=color, label=col, height=0.6)
    left += cnt[col].values
ax.set_xlabel("number of plans (1, 3, 5, 10, 20 GB)")
ax.set_title(f"Repricing actions per destination (floor {FLOOR:.0%}, max {CAP - 1:.0%} above competitors)")

The rule is simple and defensible:

  1. If a plan is above the floor margin → keep the price.
  2. If it's below the floor and the floor price is still within our competitive cap → raise to the floor price (rounded to .99).
  3. If even the floor price would make us much more expensive than competitors → pricing alone can't fix it: renegotiate the supplier, switch to per-GB-used billing, change plan sizes or validity, or keep the plan only if it is strategic (e.g. a popular destination that drives security cross-sell).

10. Takeaways¶

  • Margin lives at destination × plan level: wholesale costs differ several-fold between destinations; one global margin hides loss-makers.
  • Know the billing basis. Per-GB-used billing turns breakage into margin and makes large plans profitable; per-GB-sold billing does the opposite.
  • Position prices on a price-index vs margin map: cut where we're expensive and very profitable, raise where we're cheap and thin.
  • Channel fees matter: the app store takes a big share before data costs — steer top-ups and repeat purchases to the web where possible.
  • Run cost shocks through a floor-price rule with a competitive cap; when the floor price is uncompetitive, fix supplier terms or plan design instead of forcing a price.
  • Judge plans by profit per customer including top-ups.
